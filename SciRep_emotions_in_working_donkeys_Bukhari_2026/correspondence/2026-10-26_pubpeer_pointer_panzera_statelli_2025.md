# PubPeer entry pointed to by McElligott (reply of 2026-10-26)

Paper: Panzera M., Statelli A. (2025). Heart Rate Variability Spectral Analysis for Monitoring
the Emotional Level in a Donkey Involved in Animal-Assisted Therapy: A Single Subject Design
During Animal-Assisted Therapy Sessions. *Veterinary Sciences*, doi 10.3390/vetsci12121131.
PubPeer: https://pubpeer.com/publications/23C379C2A259C12178F53CFE3F3E7F

Comment text as it appeared on PubPeer on 2026-10-31 (copied by Alberto from the browser; the
page renders comments client-side and could not be fetched by script). Poster and date not
captured. Quoted verbatim:

> There are several potential issues with the ethics statements for both the animal and human
> components of this study
>
> The manuscript states that no ethical approval was required for the donkey because the
> procedure was "non-invasive". However, EU Directive 2010/63/EU (implemented in Italy as D.lgs.
> 26/2014) requires project authorisation for procedures that may cause pain, suffering, or
> distress, even when no invasive equipment is used.
>
> In this study, the authors report substantial sympathetic activation in the donkey during AAT
> sessions. For example, Fcmax increased from 55.19 bpm (baseline) to 75.55 bpm during sessions,
> and the authors explicitly interpret this as "emotional distress". This raises the question of
> whether the procedure indeed had the potential to cause distress and therefore should have
> required ethical oversight under Directive 2010/63/EU.
>
> A second issue concerns the human participants. The study involves ten adults diagnosed with
> paranoid schizophrenia, participating in a structured therapeutic programme with video
> recording, yet the manuscript states that "informed consent and ethical approval were not
> required". Even if no human data were analysed, research protocols involving vulnerable adults,
> video capture, or behavioural interventions typically require either ethical approval or at the
> very least, formal ethics waiver, according to standard European clinical research norms and
> GDPR requirements.
>
> These two points suggest that the ethical framework described in the manuscript may not be
> adequate or consistent with expected standards for research involving both animals and
> vulnerable human participants.
